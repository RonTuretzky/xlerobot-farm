FIELD GUIDE / NY GROWING TRIAL

Six days is enough to learn a lot.

Aim to see cress germinate, establish green seedlings, and prove that the printed parts hold and deliver water. A complete harvest is a bonus, not the acceptance test.

Your first setup, step by step

Use what you already have

  • Completed printed cress planter, or the square kit.
  • Fresh garden-cress seed. Use untreated seed intended for growing; check the delivered packet.
  • Plain, unscented household kitchen paper and clean water.
  • A clean kitchen tray/dish underneath, a small cup or spoon, and a phone camera.
  • A household loose cover if the cress planter needs humidity retention; square kit includes its cover.

No growing soil, fertilizer, purchased mats, sensors or robot needed for this short paper trial.

What “self-watering” means

The rectangular planter stores a little water below the seeds. Paper carries it upward by capillary action. You still check it and refill by hand.

The square kit has a cover and removable insert, but it does not automatically wick unless its growing medium makes the needed connection to water.

Inspect, rinse and leak-test

Remove loose plastic, rinse with cool water and check the fit without forcing clips. Put the empty trough/outer tray on dry kitchen paper inside an existing dish. Add a modest amount of water below the growing surface. Inspect after 30 minutes and again later that day. Any wet patch underneath fails the test: use another dish for growing while repairing/reprinting.

The 30-minute check is our proposed initial screen, not a leak certification. PLA prints may retain residue and are not established food-contact equipment. Treat unknown-filament prints as growth/mechanical tests; use a suitable food-growing container for any batch intended to eat. Avoid boiling water or a hot dishwasher on the PLA parts.

Make the wick actually touch the water

For the rectangular planter: snap its four insets into the holder. Feed paper down into each inset and lay 3–4 layers across the seed platform, maintaining a continuous wet-paper path. Pre-wet the paper, drain surface puddles and fill the trough below the seed platform. The creator suggests about 80% full; don’t use that as an excuse to submerge seeds.

For the square kit: put damp paper on the perforated insert inside its outer tray. Keep it moist from above initially; only rely on bottom water after confirming paper/root contact or a working wick. Water underneath an air gap cannot wet the paper automatically.

Sow a small, even patch

Scatter dry cress seeds onto moist paper, with no thick piles. Do not pre-soak cress: it becomes sticky when wet. One packet is enough for small test patches; there is no need to densely fill the entire large tray. Give both kits similar seeded patch areas and density, using the same seed lot. Keep an unseeded edge on the same sheet for moisture checks.

Use a phone note or tape already at home to label A = wick planter, B = square tray, and optionally C = household dish. A household reference can help distinguish seed trouble from a printed-container problem; it is not a controlled scientific experiment.

Keep the first stage damp, then give it light

Follow the seed packet’s conditions. A loose cover can reduce early drying; check at least morning and evening and do not seal it airtight. Remove the cover when shoots emerge and begin standing up; use the plant’s condition rather than an automatic date.

Then place it in bright natural light away from intense window heat, radiators and cold drafts. An ordinary comfortable room is a practical starting point. Leave airflow around the seedlings. Never leave a covered tray baking in direct sun.

Assembly steps adapted from Macce’s planter instructions; cress sowing/early growth from Seedmart. Our leak screens, comparisons and log schedule are proposed tests. The tray setup is an adaptation to the actual printed kit, not a claim that the seed supplier tested these prints.

A six-day observation plan

Day 0 · fit, water and sow

Complete the leak/wick checks, photograph the dry assembly and first sowing. Record crop, date, paper layers, seeded area and starting water level. Measure water with kitchen utensils only if practical; otherwise record “not measured”.

Days 1–2 · check the water path

Morning and evening: check the trough, paper at several spots, and the dry surface underneath. Add only enough water to keep paper evenly damp. Photograph from the same angle. Some seed may swell or begin rooting; slower emergence is still possible.

Days 2–4 · look for emergence

When shoots establish, uncover and give bright light. Log first roots, upright shoots and seed leaves. If a patch is dry while the trough has water, fix wick contact instead of adding more water blindly.

Days 4–5 · test useful repeatability

Compare wetness across the tray, count manual top-ups and note any leak or wilt. Practice tiny hand pours at the refill opening using your kitchen measure. Do not deliberately drought-stress the live crop; test dry/damp/wet sensor samples separately if electronics are available.

Day 6 · record and close the trial

Take final photos, compare the two kits, list changes for Japan and export the notebook. Arrange continued human care in New York or end the trial and clean/dry the prints. Do not pack live seedlings, used growing medium or remaining seed for Japan under an assumption that they are cleared.

Success before departure: visible germination, a reliable water path, no observed leaks, a known manual refill routine and photos/notes explaining failures. This does not establish autonomous care, edible-product safety or a guaranteed number of unattended days.

What a problem looks like

There is water below, but the paper is dry

The paper may not reach the reservoir, the wick may have separated, or the tray may be tilted. Re-wet and reconnect it. Check multiple points. Don’t drown the seeds to compensate for an air gap.

Seeds are floating or the surface is puddled

Remove excess water gently and lower the water level. Keep the paper damp, not submerged. Avoid blasting seeds with a direct pour.

The shoots are pale or leaning

Uncover after emergence and improve light without overheating. Use the same camera angle to track changes; brightness in a phone photo alone is not a light-meter reading.

Nothing has emerged by day 4

Check seed identity/packet guidance, freshness, paper moisture and temperature. Compare the household reference if you started one. Don’t keep increasing water; saturated seeds can also fail.

Slime, fuzzy growth or an unpleasant smell

Cress produces clear gel around wet seeds. Root hairs can look fuzzy, while spreading fuzzy patches, discoloration or a bad smell warrant stopping and discarding the affected trial. A photo alone may not distinguish them reliably. Don’t eat an uncertain batch; clean the container before restarting.
